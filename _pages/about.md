# About

I'm a master's student in Medialogy at Aalborg University Copenhagen, where I also earned my BSc in the same field. My work focuses on immersive technologies, human-computer interaction (HCI), and user-centered design, bridging academic research and creative industry practice.

My interdisciplinary background encompasses game and film development, biometric sensing, artificial intelligence, interaction design, and experimental research in virtual reality (VR). My research is situated at the intersection of interactive digital narratives, embodied interaction, and media cognition, with particular interest in how narrative structure and perceptual processes influence participants' physical and cognitive engagement with media technologies. More recently my work has turned toward the phenomenology of immersive media and biosemiotics, asking how virtual environments shape first-person lived experience.

I am currently on my 9th semester, beginning an internship at the Augmented Cognition Lab (AAU Copenhagen), where research combines first-person accounts of experience with bio-behavioral measurement.

Recent projects include *What Is It Like To Be a Tree*, an embodied VR experience in which breathing drives the growth of a forest through a mycorrhizal root network, developed in collaboration with the Augmented Cognition Lab; an affect-adaptive interactive digital narrative that responds in real time to players' physiological stress; and *Metamorphic Efforts*, a generative audiovisual translation of Kafka's *The Metamorphosis* through Laban Movement Analysis.

As a student assistant at the Creative Technology Lab (AAU CREATE), I help students and staff make the most of our lab resources while managing technical equipment. As an XR Research Assistant on the Balance VR project at the Multisensory Experience Lab, we examined the relationship between balance and motion sickness in VR. Built on work from our bachelor group projects, where we created a VIMS-inducing VR tool and published a paper at IEEE VRW 2025.
